RETROFIT: MALABAR DRIVE

It is 2026. Nigel Cook's house for K & L Wilson on Malabar Drive, Ellerslie (permit drawings, 1994) is about 30 years old. Three parallel bays run east–west: on the north, a single-storey vaulted wing with a raised living room and an open kitchen; in the middle, a double-height conservatory with a glazed gable roof, motorised vents and glazed ends, used as the family's main living space; on the south, a two-storey vaulted wing behind a long curved wall, with bedrooms, studies and a bathroom off a gallery. The house is timber-framed and single-glazed; steel is used only for the courtyard's glazed structure. It is hot in summer and cold and damp in winter.

Your job: bring it up to date. Change the fabric, the glazing, the rooms and the systems, and watch what happens to temperature, humidity, running cost and carbon. Then decide whether Cook was right that "everything lies in the application".

TASKS

There are two tasks, one at a time. Task 1: beat this house, as the 1990s Building Code minimums would have delivered it, by at least 20%. When you submit it, Task 2 unlocks: reach the 2026 Building Code reference for this house, without overheating. In the tasks the rooms stay as Cook designed them; you retrofit the fabric and systems. A separate Sandbox lets you change the rooms themselves. Each task has a budget and an embodied-carbon allowance: you cannot simply pick the best of everything. Every option shows what it adds in dollars and kg of CO₂e. Each task gives you a personal completion code. Every change you make in a task is recorded in a design log that goes with your submission.
